NO RESCUE
True stories of survival.
Archive: Wikimedia Commons (authors in the description) · Satellite: Sentinel-2 (EOX / Copernicus) · Reconstructions: No Rescue · Music: original score
Six boys were lost at sea,
and back home their funerals were held.
Fifteen months later,
a fisherman saw burnt patches
on an island nobody lived on.
Then a boy dove off the
rocks and swam to his boat.
All six were alive.
Ata,
Tonga.
A storm had wrecked their boat,
and they drifted for eight days.
They ate seabirds raw.
Then,
up on the cliffs,
they found a farm.
Taro,
bananas,
chickens.
Planted by people who had vanished.
They made fire with two sticks,
and kept it burning for over a year.
Nobody was hunted.
Nobody was killed.
But who planted that farm?
In 1863,
a slave ship anchored off Ata.
The islanders were invited
below deck to eat.
Then the crew locked the hatches.
At least a hundred and forty-four
were taken.
None are known to have come home.
A century later,
their gardens kept six boys alive.
Who really saved them?
Tell me in the comments.
Their full story is on No Rescue.
