NO RESCUE
True stories of survival.
Archive: Wikimedia Commons & NASA (authors in the description) · Satellite: Sentinel-2, NASA Blue Marble · Music: original score
Four hundred and
thirty-eight days adrift,
in an open boat.
No engine.
No sail.
No radio.
And nobody came for him.
So how does a man survive
fourteen months at sea?
November 2012.
José Salvador Alvarenga leaves the coast
of Mexico for a day or two of fishing.
With him,
Ezequiel,
twenty-two.
A storm hits.
The engine dies.
The radio goes silent.
After two days,
the search is called off.
They drink urine,
then rain,
then turtle blood.
He catches fish with his bare hands.
Four months in,
Ezequiel stops eating,
and dies.
Ships pass him by.
One crew waves back,
and keeps going.
He counts the time by the moon.
Fifteen moons.
Then,
a thin line of green.
Palm trees.
The Marshall Islands,
more than six thousand miles from home.
Nobody came for him.
In the end,
he came to them.
His full story is on No Rescue.
