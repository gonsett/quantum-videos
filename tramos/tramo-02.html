NO RESCUE
True stories of survival.
Archive: U.S. National Archives (public domain) · Satellite: Sentinel-2 (EOX / Copernicus) · Reconstructions: No Rescue · Music: original score
In 1958,
oil surveyors flying over the
Sahara spotted an American bomber.
Its radio still worked.
One of its guns still fired.
There was water on board.
And there was nobody inside.
April 1943.
Lady Be Good was flying
home to Libya in the dark,
and her crew thought they
were still over the sea.
They flew right past their own base,
and on into the desert.
Almost out of fuel,
nine men jumped.
Eight found each other on the sand.
They believed the coast was close.
So they walked north,
with half a canteen between eight men.
At midday,
a hundred and thirty degrees.
Experts later said no one could walk
more than thirty miles out there.
They walked eighty-five.
Behind them,
about twelve miles away,
their plane sat almost whole.
Water.
A working radio.
They walked for eight days.
Nobody came.
Pilots,
navigators:
with only what they knew,
would you have walked north?
Tell me in the comments.
Their full story is on No Rescue.
