Rarity, managed.
Your diamond is worth almost nothing.
In America,
the average natural engagement ring
costs about seven thousand dollars.
Try to sell it back,
and you're often offered less than half.
Meanwhile,
in a factory in China,
a machine is growing the same diamond.
Same carbon.
Same crystal.
Same fire.
Wholesale,
a one carat stone like that now sells
for about eighty-seven dollars.
So what did you really pay for?
A story.
In 1938,
De Beers,
the cartel that controlled most
of the world's rough diamonds,
hired a New York ad agency.
In 1947,
a young copywriter wrote four words.
A diamond is forever.
Forever means one thing.
Never sell it.
Because if everyone sold at once,
the price would collapse.
Then the machines got cheap.
Today,
sixty-one percent of engagement rings
in America have a lab-grown stone.
And De Beers is up for sale.
Reportedly,
for around one billion dollars.
The rarity was managed.
The price was written by an ad agency.
The full story is on the channel.
