A new country.
In three days.
On November 3rd,
1903,
Panama broke away from Colombia.
By November 6th,
the United States had recognized it.
So how do you build a country that fast?
First,
you park a warship in the harbor.
The USS Nashville reached
Colón the day before.
Then five hundred Colombian soldiers
landed to crush the revolt.
But the railroad's American superintendent put
their generals on a special
train to Panama City,
and left the troops behind.
That afternoon,
the generals were arrested.
The only person killed
was a Chinese shopkeeper,
hit by a shell from a Colombian gunboat.
Fifteen days later,
the canal treaty was signed in Washington.
Not by a Panamanian.
By a Frenchman who hadn't lived
in Panama for seventeen years.
It gave the United States a ten-mile strip,
forever.
As if it were the sovereign.
A canal built a country.
